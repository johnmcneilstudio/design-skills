# JMS Tone of Voice

Source: `johnmcneilstudio.com` (Home, About, Services), read for language patterns. This governs how JMS sounds in writing — web copy, decks, social, internal docs.

---

## 1. Core qualities

- **Declarative, not promotional.** Statements read as facts, not pitches. No exclamation points. Sentences end in periods, including headlines — this matches the typography rule in `brand-elements.md` §3 ("headline sentences end with a period"); the visual and verbal systems agree on this.
- **Plainspoken.** No agency jargon, no buzzword-stacking. Capability lists are literal nouns ("photography," "media test & learn schedules"), not invented framework names.
- **Confident without hype.** Claims are stated once, flatly, and left alone — "we are built to execute against any idea, no matter how ambitious" is as far as the site pushes; it doesn't restate or escalate the claim afterward.

## 2. Positioning by negation

The most consistent structural pattern on the site: define JMS by what it isn't, or by resolving a false tension.

- "Not an agency."
- "More than an advertising agency."
- "Where GTM approach and brand positioning work hand in hand, not against one another."

Use this pattern deliberately when writing new copy — it's a real, repeatable rhetorical move, not incidental phrasing. The structure is: **name the expected framing → invert or resolve it in the same breath.**

## 3. Sentence rhythm

- Short declarative openers, then one longer sentence that unpacks it. See the About page: one-line thesis, then a single long sentence listing everything the work "extends across."
- Comma-chained lists inside a sentence ("from the products you make, to the stories you tell, to the events you hold...") are a signature move for conveying range without a bulleted list — reserve bullets for literal capability rundowns, use the chained-comma sentence for scope/ambition statements.

## 4. Capability lists

Skill and service tags run all-lowercase, no punctuation, no bullet symbols beyond a plain dash — e.g. "brand design," "campaign creation," "vr experiences." This is a deliberate style choice distinct from body copy, which uses normal sentence case. Don't title-case these lists or add periods.

## 5. Team/people language

Describe the team as a **collective**, named by craft, not job title: "strategists, writers, designers, filmmakers, technologists, musicians and photographers" — not "our team of experts" or "cross-functional professionals." Specificity over category.

## 6. Client voice

Testimonials are left in the client's own words, unedited into marketing-speak — including informal phrasing ("They really, really, really want to help move you forward"). Don't smooth these into polished copy; the rawness is doing work.

---

## Do / Don't

**Do**
- End headlines with a period.
- Invert an expected framing in the same sentence you raise it.
- Use comma-chained sentences for scope claims; bullets only for literal capability lists.
- Name disciplines by craft ("filmmakers," "photographers"), not generic titles.

**Don't**
- Use exclamation points.
- Stack superlatives — say the strong claim once, don't reinforce it.
- Title-case or punctuate capability tag lists.
- Polish client quotes into brand voice.
