# JMS Philosophy

Source: `johnmcneilstudio.com` (Home, About, Services) — current — plus the studio-identity core from `JMS_BrandGuidelines_v21.pdf`, which still holds.

---

## 1. What we are

A studio, not an agency. This has been the position since 2009 and hasn't moved.

The word "agency" implies a fixed lane — campaigns, ads, a defined deliverable. JMS defines itself by the opposite: work that extends across every surface a company has to represent itself — the product, the story, the event, the people it hires, and everything in between. Nothing is out of scope if it touches the brand.

## 2. The core belief

Creativity is the catalyst that drives business forward.

Not decoration on top of a business strategy — the mechanism that moves it. A catalyst creates a reaction while staying true to itself: JMS creates change in a client's business through creative work, while holding a consistent, recognizable identity of its own. That's the throughline between the 2021 manifesto and the current site — the language changed, the belief didn't.

## 3. How that becomes an org

The studio expresses this belief through four disciplines, working as one team rather than four silos:

- **Strategy** — creative thinking, cultural understanding, and business acumen combined, not separated.
- **Creative** — turning strategic insight into campaigns and content built to break through, not blend in.
- **Production** — execution against any idea "no matter how ambitious," from 3D and motion to full digital development.
- **Media** — insight-driven distribution so the work actually reaches the audience it was built for.

The pitch is that these normally live in different vendors; at JMS they live under one roof.

## 4. Who does the work

Not a roster of job titles — a collective: strategists, writers, designers, filmmakers, technologists, musicians, photographers. The range of disciplines is treated as a feature of the brand, not incidental — it's direct evidence for the "we do everything a brand touches" claim in §1.

## 5. What clients say this feels like

Recurring theme across client quotes: JMS is hired to think holistically about a business problem, not to execute a pre-defined deliverable. Clients describe being pushed past what they expected to ask for, not just served what they asked for. That's the practical proof of the "catalyst" belief in §2 — the studio is credited with reframing the problem, not just solving the stated one.

---

## What NOT to do with this philosophy

- Don't shrink it back down to an "agency" framing, even informally — the entire positioning is built on the negation.
- Don't present the four disciplines as separable vendors/silos — the "one roof" claim is load-bearing.
- Don't lean on the 2009 founding date or "not an agency since 2009" as a nostalgia beat — it's a credibility marker (this has held up), not a legacy story.
