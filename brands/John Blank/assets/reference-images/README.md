## Architectural constraints

- The storefront is one continuous bay: a broad main doorway and an adjacent window into the same room.
- Do not invent a vestibule, divider, second storefront, or additional depth.
- Preserve the black inset storefront, white facade/mural surfaces, raised black forecourt, sidewalk, tree, utility pole, and neighboring architecture.
- Preserve the front-room relationship to the raised wood gallery and its three broad connecting steps.

## Reference hierarchy

When references conflict, use this order of authority:

1. Measured drawings and new site measurements.
2. Real photographs for visible architecture and spatial relationships.
3. Documented measurements, with their stated uncertainty.
4. Creative direction.
5. Generated concepts.

Generated images are creative explorations, never architectural source-of-truth. Photographs establish visible conditions; do not infer exact dimensions from perspective alone.

## Usage and scale checks

- Use the relevant real photograph as the starting reference for each view, and cross-check adjoining spaces and street context against the other photographs.
- Check concept scale against the approximate room width, depth, ceiling heights, and level change before accepting a generated view.
- Keep proposed graphics, furnishings, and installations consistent with the existing openings, room proportions, and architectural constraints.
- Flag missing or conflicting dimensions for measurement instead of inventing precise values.
